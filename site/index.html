A binary memory whose accuracy is computed before it is built.

ABM stores (subject, relation, object) facts in one deterministic binary trace. From the dimension and the facts it will hold, abm.exact computes its expected accuracy, a mean over queries, with no fitted parameter. That is the only thing measured in its favour.

USE

from abm import Memory, exact

triples = [("payment_service", "requires", "auth_service"),
           ("auth_service", "writes_to", "session_store")]

exact.contract_for(triples, dim=4096)  # expected accuracy, alias ceiling
exact.min_dimension(triples, 0.9)      # smallest D predicted to reach 90%

mem = Memory(dim=4096)
for t in triples:
    mem.store(*t)
mem.chain("payment_service", ["requires", "writes_to"])
# ("session_store", 0.99…)  confidence ranks answers, NOT calibrated

LIMITS, FIRST

Not denseAt equal bits an exact store recalls more in 14/14 cells and a Bloom filter makes fewer membership errors (preregistered test 18).
The algebra buys nothingOn two-hop chains a dict + join of the same bits is at least as accurate in every cell (test 19).
SlowIn Python, 2 600× to 229 725× slower per question than a dict (test 19).
A mean, not a guaranteeThe computed accuracy is a mean over queries. Cycles in the graph break the model by up to 6 points; an exact store's accuracy is computable too, more simply.

ABM is of use only where a distributed binary representation is required anyway.

WHY TRUST THE COMPUTATION

The exact model was tested by nineteen preregistrations: 0.27 points of error at an unseen dimension, 0.6–1.1 points on dense subgraphs of two real knowledge graphs. Failures are kept in the record, next to the successes.
